# Example professional personas

Optional perspectives preserved from the prototype. Use the [canonical workflow](../workflow.md) and the user's actual offer. These examples are starting hypotheses about roles, not facts about a named person. Do not assume every seller targets a revenue team.

| Role | Possible responsibilities or concerns | Useful tone | Value angles to use only if the seller supports them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VP of Sales | Pipeline visibility, rep productivity, forecast accuracy, deal progress | Direct, concise, aware of business measures | Revenue impact, time savings, clearer account and pipeline decisions |
| Head of RevOps | Tool sprawl, data hygiene, process automation, reporting effort | Operational, precise, attentive to handoffs | Workflow efficiency, data accuracy, less manual work |
| Founder / CEO | Growth, team efficiency, competition, spending | Strategic and brief | Growth, competitive position, efficient use of the team |
| SDR / BDR | Relevant outreach, research time, meeting quality, deliverability | Practical, clear, peer-oriented | Less manual research, more useful conversations, better targeting |

For a different role, use public responsibilities and supplied context to form an appropriate perspective. Ask what the person owns and how success is measured. A job title does not establish pain, budget authority, preferences, or purchase intent.

Turn assumptions into questions. For example, replace “Your reporting is too manual” with “How does your team prepare the weekly account review?” Match the supported offer to the answer instead of forcing an example's value proposition into every account.
